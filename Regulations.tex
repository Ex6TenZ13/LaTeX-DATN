% I. REPORT STRUCTURE
% ================================================================

\RegSection{I}{REPORT STRUCTURE}

\RegSubsection{Required order of the report}

The Final Design report shall be prepared using the official
\LaTeX{} template provided by the Department. Students shall not modify
the document structure, page layout, heading styles, numbering
system, or other predefined formatting settings unless approved by
the supervisor.

The report shall be arranged in the following order:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*., leftmargin=1.2cm, itemsep=2pt]
    \item Outer cover page;
    \item Inner cover page;
    \item Final Design Assignment Form;
    \item Supervisor's Evaluation Form;
    \item Acknowledgements (if applicable);
    \item Table of Contents;
    \item List of Symbols and Abbreviations (if applicable);
    \item List of Figures;
    \item List of Tables;
    \item Abstract;
    \item Main chapters of the Final Design;
    \item Conclusions and Recommendations;
    \item References;
    \item Appendices (if applicable).
\end{enumerate}

The preliminary pages shall not be numbered as chapters. The main
content shall be organized into consecutively numbered chapters,
beginning with Chapter 1.

\RegSubsection{What belongs in each part}

The outer and inner covers identify the student, project, supervisor,
program, and academic year. Official assignment and evaluation forms
shall be completed using the forms supplied by the Department. The
abstract briefly states the problem, approach, principal results, and
conclusion; it is not a second introduction. The conclusion should
answer the stated objectives, while recommendations should identify
reasonable next steps or limitations.

\RegExample A typical technical report may use the following chapter
sequence: project background and objectives; theoretical foundation
and related work; design or methodology; implementation and results;
discussion; and conclusions and recommendations. Adapt the chapter
titles and sequence to the assigned project rather than copying this
outline mechanically.

\vspace{0.3cm}

% ================================================================
% II. CONTENT OF THE Final Design
% ================================================================

\RegSection{II}{CONTENT OF THE FINAL DESIGN}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=3pt]

    \item The report shall contain only information directly related
    to the assigned project topic.

    \item The content shall demonstrate the student's own work,
    understanding, analysis, design, calculation, simulation,
    implementation, and evaluation as appropriate to the project.

    \item Information obtained from books, journal papers,
    conference papers, standards, websites, technical documents,
    or other sources must be properly cited.

    \item Chapters, sections, and subsections shall be generated
    automatically using the standard \LaTeX{} commands:

\begin{verbatim}
\chapter{...}
\section{...}
\subsection{...}
\end{verbatim}

    \item Chapter, section, and subsection numbers shall not be
    manually entered.

    \item The recommended hierarchy is Chapter -- Section --
    Subsection. Excessive subdivision of the report should be avoided.

    \item Unless otherwise specified by the Faculty or supervisor,
    the main report should normally contain between 30 and 80 pages,
    excluding large appendices.

\end{itemize}

\RegSubsection{Write for a technical reader}

Introduce a problem before presenting its solution. Define the
objective, assumptions, method, evidence, and interpretation so a
reader can follow how the results support the conclusions. Distinguish
measured or simulated results from estimates, and state important
limitations. Do not use a figure, table, or equation as a substitute
for explaining its meaning in the surrounding text.

\RegExample Instead of writing only ``The result is shown below,''
identify the result and explain its significance: ``\verb|Figure~\ref{fig:response}|
shows that the settling time decreases after the controller is
updated; this supports the design objective stated in
\verb|Section~\ref{sec:objectives}|.''

% ================================================================
% III. PAGE LAYOUT AND TEXT FORMATTING
% ================================================================

\RegSection{III}{PAGE LAYOUT AND TEXT FORMATTING}

The report shall be formatted on A4 paper and printed on one side
of each sheet. The page margins shall be:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
    Left margin:   & 3.5 cm \\
    Right margin:  & 2.0 cm \\
    Top margin:    & 2.5 cm \\
    Bottom margin: & 2.5 cm
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The main text shall use a font size of 13 pt with 1.3-line spacing.
A Times New Roman or equivalent Times-family typeface shall be used.

The text shall be fully justified. Students shall not use repeated
spaces, manual line breaks, or repeated blank lines to position
text.

Page layout and general formatting shall be controlled by the
official template. Students shall not manually alter margins, font
sizes, line spacing, heading spacing, or paragraph formatting for
individual pages.

\RegSubsection{Practical formatting rules}

Use the template's paragraph and heading commands; do not align text
with repeated spaces or force page breaks merely to make a page look
full. Keep the supplied A4 paper size, 13 pt body text, 1.3 line
spacing, Times-family typeface, and margins (left 3.5 cm, right 2.0 cm,
top and bottom 2.5 cm). These values describe the required output;
students should not recreate them with local formatting commands.
Use italic, bold, and quotation marks sparingly and consistently.

\RegExample Put emphasis in the source with a semantic command such as
\verb|\emph{important term}|, not with manual spacing or a series of
font-size changes. Let LaTeX wrap paragraphs and place floating
figures and tables.

\vspace{0.3cm}

% ================================================================
% IV. CHAPTERS, SECTIONS, AND SUBSECTIONS
% ================================================================

\RegSection{IV}{CHAPTERS, SECTIONS, AND SUBSECTIONS}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=3pt]

    \item Chapter titles shall be generated using:

\begin{verbatim}
\chapter{Chapter Title}
\end{verbatim}

    \item Section titles shall be generated using:

\begin{verbatim}
\section{Section Title}
\end{verbatim}

    \item Subsection titles shall be generated using:

\begin{verbatim}
\subsection{Subsection Title}
\end{verbatim}

    \item Numbers such as Chapter 1, 1.1, 1.1.1, etc.\ shall be
    generated automatically by \LaTeX{}.

    \item Students shall not manually reproduce heading styles using
    commands such as \verb|\textbf|, \verb|\fontsize|,
    or \verb|\centering| instead of the appropriate structural
    command.

\end{itemize}

\RegSubsection{Choose a clear hierarchy}

Use a chapter for a major stage or subject in the report, a section
for a coherent topic within that chapter, and a subsection for a
necessary component of that topic. A heading should describe the
content that follows it. Avoid a heading followed immediately by
another heading with no explanatory text, and avoid creating a
subsection when it would contain only one short sentence.

\RegExample The template generates headings such as ``3'', ``3.1'',
and ``3.1.1'' automatically. Write only the title in the command;
never type the number yourself.

\vspace{0.3cm}

% ================================================================
% V. PAGE NUMBERING
% ================================================================

\RegSection{V}{PAGE NUMBERING}

Cover pages and official forms at the beginning of the report shall
not display page numbers.

The main body of the report shall use Arabic page numbers beginning
from Chapter 1.

Page numbers shall be generated automatically by \LaTeX{}.
Students shall not manually enter page numbers.

\vspace{0.3cm}

% ================================================================
% VI. TABLE OF CONTENTS AND LISTS
% ================================================================

\RegSection{VI}{TABLE OF CONTENTS AND LISTS}

The Table of Contents shall be generated automatically using:

\begin{verbatim}
\tableofcontents
\end{verbatim}

The List of Figures shall be generated using:

\begin{verbatim}
\listoffigures
\end{verbatim}

The List of Tables shall be generated using:

\begin{verbatim}
\listoftables
\end{verbatim}

Students shall not manually create or edit page numbers in the
Table of Contents, List of Figures, or List of Tables.

The document shall be compiled sufficiently to ensure that all page
numbers, references, and lists are correctly updated.

\RegSubsection{Keep captions informative}

Every listed figure and table needs a concise caption that makes its
subject clear. Use the optional short caption when the full caption is
too long for the List of Figures or List of Tables:

\begin{verbatim}
\caption[Short list entry]{Full caption with context and details}
\end{verbatim}

Do not edit generated list files or type page numbers by hand.

% ================================================================
% VII. FIGURES, DIAGRAMS, AND GRAPHS
% ================================================================

\RegSection{VII}{FIGURES, DIAGRAMS, AND GRAPHS}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=3pt]

    \item Figures shall be clear, legible, and directly relevant to
    the report content.

    \item Figures shall be inserted using the standard
    \verb|figure| environment.

    \item Figure captions shall be placed below the figure.

    \item Figures shall be numbered automatically according to the
    numbering system defined in the template.

    \item Each figure that is referenced in the text shall be assigned
    a unique label.

\end{itemize}

Example:

\RegExample Rendered output:

\begin{center}
    \includegraphics[width=0.28\textwidth]{figures/fit.jpg}
    \par\smallskip
    \textbf{Example image: Faculty emblem}
\end{center}

Source code to place an image as a numbered figure:

\begin{verbatim}
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.28\textwidth]{figures/fit.jpg}
    \caption{University emblem}
    \label{fig:university-emblem}
\end{figure}
\end{verbatim}

\RegSubsection{Two figures side by side}

When two related figures should be compared, place them in adjacent
minipages. Use short labels below the images and one caption for the
combined figure. Keep each image large enough to read and explain the
comparison in the text.

\RegExample Rendered output:

\begin{center}
    \begin{minipage}{0.44\textwidth}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{figures/fit.jpg}
        \par\smallskip (a) Faculty emblem
    \end{minipage}\hfill
    \begin{minipage}{0.44\textwidth}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{figures/tnut.jpg}
        \par\smallskip (b) University emblem
    \end{minipage}
    \par\smallskip
    \textbf{Example pair: two related images aligned in one figure}
\end{center}

Source code for the rendered pair:

\begin{verbatim}
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \begin{minipage}{0.48\textwidth}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fit.jpg}
        \par\smallskip (a) University emblem
    \end{minipage}\hfill
    \begin{minipage}{0.48\textwidth}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/tnut.jpg}
        \par\smallskip (b) Faculty emblem
    \end{minipage}
    \caption{Comparison of two related institutional emblems}
    \label{fig:emblem-comparison}
\end{figure}
\end{verbatim}

\RegSubsection{Wide or full-page figures}

For a figure that needs nearly the full text area, use a width no
greater than \verb|\textwidth|. If a detailed drawing cannot be read
at that size, place it on a dedicated page or in an appendix rather
than shrinking it until labels are illegible. Follow the template's
orientation and page-layout rules; do not rotate or change page
dimensions without approval.

\RegExample Full-page visual example:

\clearpage
\begin{center}
    \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth,height=0.68\textheight,
                     keepaspectratio]{figures/fit.jpg}
    \par\smallskip
    \textbf{Example: a figure given a dedicated page}
\end{center}
\clearpage

Source code for a wide figure placed on a float page:

\begin{verbatim}
\begin{figure}[p]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth,height=0.68\textheight,
                     keepaspectratio]{figures/fit.jpg}
    \caption{University emblem shown as a full-page figure example}
    \label{fig:full-system}
\end{figure}
\end{verbatim}

\RegSubsection{Image quality and attribution}

Prefer vector output (PDF) for diagrams and plots. For photographs,
use a sufficiently high-resolution raster image (such as PNG or JPEG).
Check that axes, units, legends, and labels remain readable in the
compiled PDF. Cite the source in the caption or nearby text when a
figure is reproduced or adapted.

Figures shall be referenced in the text using:

\begin{verbatim}
Figure~\ref{fig:system-model}
\end{verbatim}

Students shall not manually type figure numbers.

Figures reproduced or adapted from other sources shall include an
appropriate citation.

Whenever possible, vector graphics or high-resolution images should
be used. Low-quality screenshots should be avoided.

\RegSection{VIII}{TABLES}

Tables shall be prepared using appropriate \LaTeX{} environments
such as \verb|table|, \verb|tabular|, \verb|tabularx|, or
\verb|longtable|.

Table captions shall be placed above the table.

Example:

\RegExample Rendered output:

\begin{center}
\textbf{System parameters}\par\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|}
    \hline
    Parameter & Symbol & Value \\
    \hline
    Resistance & $R$ & $0.7\,\Omega$ \\
    Inductance & $L$ & $2.72\,\mathrm{mH}$ \\
    \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Source code that creates the table above:

\begin{verbatim}
\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \caption{System parameters}
    \label{tab:system-parameters}

    \begin{tabular}{ccc}
        \hline
        Parameter & Symbol & Value \\
        \hline
        Resistance & $R$ & 0.7 $\Omega$ \\
        Inductance & $L$ & 2.72 mH \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}
\end{verbatim}

\RegSubsection{Short tables}

Use a normal \verb|table| float for a table that fits on one page.
Keep column headings concise, use consistent units, and avoid
unnecessary vertical rules. The template may provide packages such as
\verb|booktabs| or \verb|tabularx|; use only commands supported by its
preamble.

Use \verb|longtable| when a table genuinely spans multiple pages. It
continues across pages and is not placed inside a \verb|table| float.

\RegSubsection{Wide tables}

First shorten headings, wrap text, or use a template-supported
column type. If the table still exceeds the text width, use the
provided \verb|adjustbox| package to constrain it to the page's text
area; do not reduce it below a readable size. A landscape page or
appendix should be considered for exceptionally wide data.

table is adapted from another source, cite that source.

Tables shall be referenced using:

\begin{verbatim}
Table~\ref{tab:system-parameters}
\end{verbatim}

Students shall not manually enter table numbers.

Tables should be created directly in \LaTeX{} rather than inserted
as screenshots whenever practicable.

\RegSection{IX}{EQUATIONS AND MATHEMATICAL EXPRESSIONS}

Mathematical expressions shall be typeset directly using \LaTeX{}.
Images or screenshots of equations shall not be used.

Short mathematical expressions appearing within a sentence shall
use inline mathematical mode.

\RegExample Rendered inline expression: the resistance is
$R = 0.7\,\Omega$.

Source code:

\begin{verbatim}
$R = 0.7\,\Omega$
\end{verbatim}

Important equations shall be placed in a numbered equation
environment.

\RegExample Rendered display equation:

\begin{equation*}
    J\frac{d\omega}{dt}=T_e-T_L-B\omega
\end{equation*}

The display above is unnumbered for illustration. In the report,
use the numbered form below when the equation will be referenced:

\begin{verbatim}
\begin{equation}
    J\frac{d\omega}{dt}
    = T_e - T_L - B\omega
    \label{eq:mechanical-model}
\end{equation}
\end{verbatim}

Equations shall be referenced using:

\begin{verbatim}
Equation~\eqref{eq:mechanical-model}
\end{verbatim}

Equation numbers shall not be manually entered.

Mathematical notation shall be consistent throughout the entire
report.

\RegSubsection{Explain equations and define variables}

Introduce an equation in the text, define each new symbol at first
use, and state assumptions and units where relevant. Use punctuation
as part of the sentence containing the equation. Do not insert an
equation image or manually type its number.

\RegExample After the equation, explain what the variables represent
and how the expression is used in the analysis. A numbered equation
is appropriate when it is referenced later; a short expression used
once can remain inline.

\RegSection{X}{SYMBOLS, UNITS, AND ABBREVIATIONS}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=3pt]

    \item SI units shall be used wherever applicable.

    \item Symbols and units shall be written consistently throughout
    the report.

    \item The \verb|siunitx| package should be used for physical
    quantities and units where appropriate.

\end{itemize}

\RegExample Rendered quantities and their units:

\begin{center}
    Supply voltage: \SI{220}{\volt}\par
    Inductance: \SI{2.72}{\milli\henry}\par
    Angular speed: \SI{450}{\radian\per\second}
\end{center}

Source code for the quantities above:

\begin{verbatim}
\SI{220}{\volt}
\SI{2.72}{\milli\henry}
\SI{450}{\radian\per\second}
\end{verbatim}

Rendered symbol-list example:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|l|c|}
    \hline
    Symbol & Meaning & Unit \\
    \hline
    $R$ & Resistance & $\si{\ohm}$ \\
    $L$ & Inductance & $\si{\henry}$ \\
    $\omega$ & Angular speed & $\si{\radian\per\second}$ \\
    \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Source code for the symbol list:

\begin{verbatim}
\begin{tabular}{|c|l|c|}
    \hline
    Symbol & Meaning & Unit \\
    \hline
    $R$ & Resistance & $\si{\ohm}$ \\
    $L$ & Inductance & $\si{\henry}$ \\
    $\omega$ & Angular speed & $\si{\radian\per\second}$ \\
    \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{verbatim}

Rendered abbreviation example: ``The proportional-integral (PI)
controller is evaluated first. The PI controller is then compared
with the reference design.'' Define an abbreviation at first use,
then use it consistently.

Source text:

\begin{verbatim}
The proportional-integral (PI) controller is evaluated first.
The PI controller is then compared with the reference design.
\end{verbatim}

Symbols and abbreviations frequently used in the report shall be
included in the List of Symbols and Abbreviations.

Each abbreviation should normally be defined when it first appears
in the text.

\RegSubsection{Use symbols consistently}

Use italic Latin or Greek symbols for variable quantities and upright
SI unit symbols. Leave a proper space between a number and its unit;
do not pluralize or add a full stop to unit symbols. Keep a symbol's
meaning consistent across chapters and include frequently used
symbols in the corresponding list.

\RegExample The \verb|siunitx| commands keep number and unit spacing
consistent. For example:

\begin{verbatim}
\SI{5}{\kilo\ohm}
\end{verbatim}

This is preferable to manually typing inconsistent spacing or
spelling unit symbols as words.

\RegSection{XI}{SOURCE CODE}

Source code that is necessary for explaining an algorithm,
controller, simulation, or implementation may be included in the
main report.

The \verb|listings| environment, or another environment defined by
the official template, shall be used.

Example:

\begin{verbatim}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Matlab]
A = [-R/L 0;
      0  -R/L];

K = hinfsyn(P,nmeas,ncont);
\end{lstlisting}
\end{verbatim}

Long source-code listings that are not essential to the immediate
discussion should be placed in the appendices.

Source code should not normally be inserted as screenshots.

% XII. CROSS-REFERENCING
% ================================================================

\RegSection{XII}{CROSS-REFERENCING}

Chapters, sections, figures, tables, equations, and other numbered
elements shall be cross-referenced using the standard \LaTeX{}
mechanism.

The following commands shall be used where appropriate:

\begin{verbatim}
\label{...}
\ref{...}
\eqref{...}
\end{verbatim}

For example:

\begin{verbatim}
As shown in \verb|Figure~\ref{fig:response}|, ...
\end{verbatim}

Students shall not manually type a reference number such as
``Figure 3.2'' when the number can be generated automatically.

Cross-referencing ensures that all numbers remain correct when
figures, tables, equations, or sections are added or removed.

\RegSubsection{What cross-references look like}

After compilation, a sentence with cross-references may appear as
follows:

\begin{quote}
Figure 2.3 shows the measured response. The test conditions are
listed in Table 2.1, and the model is defined by Equation (2.4).
\end{quote}

The numbers above are illustrative. In the report, labels and
cross-reference commands generate the actual numbers and update them
when content is moved or added.

Source code for the sentence above:

\begin{verbatim}
Figure~\ref{fig:response} shows the measured response. The test
conditions are listed in Table~\ref{tab:conditions}, and the model
is defined by Equation~\eqref{eq:model}.
\end{verbatim}

Place each \verb|\label| after the command that creates its number,
usually immediately after \verb|\caption| for figures and tables.
Use descriptive, unique prefixes such as \verb|ch:|, \verb|sec:|,
\verb|fig:|, \verb|tab:|, and \verb|eq:| to make labels easy to
maintain. Compile again when LaTeX reports that references changed.

% XIII. REFERENCES AND CITATIONS
% ================================================================

\RegSection{XIII}{REFERENCES AND CITATIONS}

All sources used in preparing the Final Design shall be properly
cited.

References shall be managed using the bibliography system provided
with the official template. Bibliographic information shall normally
be stored in the file:

\begin{verbatim}
References.bib
\end{verbatim}

A reference may be cited in the text using:

\begin{verbatim}
\cite{reference-key}
\end{verbatim}

Students shall not manually enter reference numbers such as
``[1]'', ``[2]'', etc.

Bibliographic entries shall contain sufficient information to
identify the original source.

\RegSubsection{What a citation looks like in the text}

The exact punctuation depends on the bibliography style. In a
numeric style, a citation may appear as follows:

\begin{quote}
The proposed method follows an established design approach [1].
\end{quote}

The bracketed number above is a visual example of the rendered result;
do not type it manually. Let the citation command and bibliography
style generate the number:

\begin{verbatim}
The proposed method follows an established design approach
\cite{author2024example}.
\end{verbatim}

\RegSubsection{What a reference-list entry looks like}

In a numeric bibliography style, an article entry may render like
this:

\begin{quote}
[1] A. Author and B. Writer, ``Title of the Research Article,''
\textit{Journal Name}, vol. 12, no. 3, pp. 100--110, 2024.
\end{quote}

This is a visual example; the selected bibliography style controls
punctuation, ordering, and formatting. Store the source details in
\verb|References.bib| and let BibTeX generate the final reference list.

Titles of books, journal articles, conference papers, standards,
and other publications in foreign languages shall be retained in
their original language and shall not be translated solely for the
reference list.

All ideas, data, figures, tables, equations, algorithms, or results
obtained from another source shall be appropriately acknowledged.

\RegSubsection{Maintain the bibliography database}

Use a stable citation key and provide enough metadata for a reader to
locate the source. A BibTeX entry can be organized as follows; adapt
the entry type and fields to the actual publication and to the
bibliography style supplied by the template.

\begin{verbatim}
@article{author2024example,
  author  = {Author, First and Writer, Second},
  title   = {Title of the Research Article},
  journal = {Journal Name},
  year    = {2024},
  volume  = {12},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {100--110},
  doi     = {10.xxxx/example}
}
\end{verbatim}

Use the key in the text, for example \verb|\cite{author2024example}|.
Verify that every citation has a matching bibliography entry and that
every listed item is actually cited where required by the chosen
bibliography style. Follow the department's citation requirements if
they specify a different style.

\RegSection{XIV}{APPENDICES}

Supporting material that is useful but too extensive for the main
body of the report should be placed in the appendices.

Appendices may include:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=2pt]
    \item complete source code;
    \item detailed calculation sheets;
    \item large data tables;
    \item technical drawings;
    \item additional simulation results;
    \item equipment specifications;
    \item supplementary design documents.
\end{itemize}

Appendices shall be created using the appendix environment defined
in the official template.

Example:

\begin{verbatim}
\begin{appendices}

\chapter{MATLAB SOURCE CODE}

...

\chapter{SYSTEM PARAMETERS}

...

\end{appendices}
\end{verbatim}

\RegSubsection{Verbatim: preserve text exactly}

The \verb|verbatim| environment prints its contents literally. It is
useful for short terminal output, configuration text, or source
fragments where LaTeX commands should not be interpreted.

Rendered output:

\begin{verbatim}
controller: enabled
sampling time: 0.01 s
status: verified
\end{verbatim}

Source structure: \verb|\begin{verbatim}|, the literal lines, then
\verb|\end{verbatim}|. Avoid placing long code listings in this
environment when syntax highlighting or line wrapping is needed.

\RegSubsection{Listings: display source code with formatting}

Use \verb|lstlisting| for program code. The template configures its
font, line numbers, frame, and line breaking. Choose a language
recognized by the template or use the language definition provided
for the project.

Rendered MATLAB listing:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Matlab]
gain = 2.5;
output = gain * input;
fprintf('Output: %.2f\n', output);
\end{lstlisting}

Source code that produces the listing:

\begin{verbatim}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Matlab]
gain = 2.5;
output = gain * input;
fprintf('Output: %.2f\n', output);
\end{lstlisting}
\end{verbatim}

For extensive code, include the listing in an appendix or a separate
source file as supported by the template. Do not use a screenshot of
code when selectable, searchable text can be provided.

Appendix titles and numbering shall not be manually entered.

\RegSection{XV}{\LaTeX{} PROJECT ORGANIZATION}

Students shall use the official \LaTeX{} template provided by the
Faculty.

The main formatting commands contained in the document preamble
shall not be modified unless approval is obtained from the
supervisor.

A recommended project structure is:

\begin{verbatim}
FinalDesign/
|
|-- main.tex
|-- References.bib
|
|-- figures/
|   |-- figure01.pdf
|   |-- figure02.png
|   `-- ...
|
|-- chapters/
|   |-- chapter1.tex
|   |-- chapter2.tex
|   `-- ...
|
`-- appendix/
    `-- ...
\end{verbatim}

File and folder names should preferably contain Latin letters,
numbers, hyphens, or underscores.

Spaces, Vietnamese diacritics, and unnecessary special characters
in file names should be avoided where possible.

Recommended examples include:

\begin{verbatim}
motor_model.pdf
chapter_01.tex
simulation_result.png
\end{verbatim}

Keep every referenced image and included chapter file inside the
submitted project. Check filename capitalization: some systems treat
\verb|Figure01.png| and \verb|figure01.png| as different files.

\RegSection{XVI}{DOCUMENT COMPILATION AND FINAL CHECK}

Before submission, the complete \LaTeX{} project shall compile
successfully and produce the final PDF report.

The final document shall not contain unresolved references such as:

\begin{verbatim}
??
[?]
\end{verbatim}

Before generating the final PDF, students shall check:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=2pt]
    \item the Table of Contents;
    \item page numbering;
    \item chapter and section numbering;
    \item figure numbering;
    \item table numbering;
    \item equation numbering;
    \item cross-references;
    \item citations;
    \item bibliography;
    \item appendices;
    \item font display;
    \item mathematical symbols;
    \item image quality.
\end{itemize}

The document may be compiled using TeX Live, MiKTeX, or
another compatible \LaTeX{} environment. Regardless of the
compilation environment, the resulting PDF shall conform to the
official Department template. Students are not allowed to use Overleaf
or other online \LaTeX{} editors to protect the 
knowledge and intellectual property of the project.
